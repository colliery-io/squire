# Materials Guide

Version: 1.0

Purpose:

This document defines the material system for Squire and establishes how materials communicate meaning throughout the application.

This guide exists to ensure that every icon, banner, card, badge, reward, illustration, and decorative element uses a consistent visual language.

Materials are not chosen arbitrarily.

Each material has a specific purpose and communicates a specific meaning.

---

# Design Philosophy

Squire is not a fantasy game.

Squire is a real-world reward system presented through the lens of a child-friendly fantasy adventure.

Children complete real-world tasks.

Parents create real-world rewards.

Fantasy provides motivation, progression, excitement, and imagination.

Because of this:

Quests should feel fantastical.

Rewards should feel tangible.

Progress should feel meaningful.

Nothing should feel dark, dangerous, or intimidating.

---

# Visual Style

Target Style:

Cozy Storybook Fantasy

Think:

* D&D for Kids
* Hearthstone
* Storybook adventure journals
* Fantasy quest boards

Avoid:

* Dark fantasy
* Grim fantasy
* Horror themes
* Dungeon crawler aesthetics
* Hyper-realistic rendering

The world should feel:

* Warm
* Welcoming
* Handcrafted
* Familiar
* Encouraging

# Material Ownership

Each material owns a specific responsibility within the design system.

---

## Parchment Owns Information

Purpose:

Display content.

Examples:

* Quest cards
* Reward cards
* Dialogs
* Forms
* Tooltips
* Quest descriptions

Characteristics:

* Warm
* Readable
* Welcoming

Parchment should always prioritize readability.

---

## Wood Owns Structure

Purpose:

Frame information.

Examples:

* Borders
* Card frames
* Signs
* Section dividers
* Decorative framing

Characteristics:

* Rounded
* Handcrafted
* Friendly

Wood provides structure without demanding attention.

---

## Purple Cloth Owns Hierarchy

Purpose:

Direct attention.

Examples:

* Headers
* Navigation
* Hero banners
* Section titles
* Important labels

Characteristics:

* Rich
* Recognizable
* Branded

Purple cloth is the primary visual identity of Squire.

---

## Stone Owns Milestones

Purpose:

Represent permanence and accomplishment.

Examples:

* Achievement displays
* Major badges
* Long-term progression
* Milestone rewards

Characteristics:

* Stable
* Durable
* Respectable

Stone should feel earned.

---

## Gold Owns Progress

Purpose:

Represent earned value.

Examples:

* Coins
* Progress indicators
* Counters
* Reward costs
* Positive feedback

Characteristics:

* Warm
* Valuable
* Rewarding

Important:

Gold represents progress.

Gold does not represent rarity.

Gold does not represent prestige.

---

## Gems Own Achievement

Purpose:

Represent exceptional accomplishments.

Examples:

* Long streaks
* Habit milestones
* Behavioral achievements
* Special progression systems

Characteristics:

* Rare
* Valuable
* Memorable

Gems should never feel common.

A single gem should feel meaningful.

---

## Purple and Orange Own Prestige

Purpose:

Represent exceptional accomplishments.

Examples:

* Legendary badges
* Exceptional milestones
* Major achievement displays

Characteristics:

* Rare
* Celebratory
* Prestigious

Purple and orange combinations should appear sparingly.

Prestige loses value if it becomes common.

---

# Physical Objects vs Currency

Squire intentionally separates rewards from progression.

---

## Physical Objects

Represent tangible things.

Examples:

* Scrolls
* Chests
* Badges
* Books
* Certificates
* Tickets
* Crowns
* Trophies

These objects should feel real and touchable.

Children should feel that they represent something waiting for them in the real world.

---

## Currency Objects

Represent progress.

Examples:

* Coins
* Gems

These are symbolic.

They measure achievement.

They are not the reward itself.

---

# Currency System

---

## Coins

Purpose:

Short-term progression.

Loop:

Complete Quest
→ Earn Coin
→ Redeem Reward

Characteristics:

* Common
* Frequent
* Motivating

Coins should feel valuable but abundant.

---

## Gems

Purpose:

Long-term achievement.

Loop:

Build Habit
→ Earn Gem
→ Unlock Special Opportunities

Characteristics:

* Rare
* Significant
* Memorable

Examples:

* 30-day reading streak
* 60-day cleaning streak
* 90-day exercise streak

Gems should never feel disposable.

---

# Gem Philosophy

Version 1 uses pure gemstones.

Examples:

* Ruby
* Emerald
* Sapphire
* Amethyst

Gems are not:

* Characters
* Mascots
* Creatures
* Animated objects

Future versions may expand the system.

Version 1 should remain simple.

---

# Material Pairing Rules

Excellent Pairings:

* Parchment + Wood
* Parchment + Purple Cloth
* Purple Cloth + Gold
* Stone + Gold
* Gold + Gem
* Purple + Orange

Avoid Excessive Use Of:

* Gold + Gold
* Gem + Gem
* Stone + Stone

Visual contrast creates hierarchy.

Hierarchy creates clarity.

---

# Texture Philosophy

Textures should support the experience.

Textures should never dominate the experience.

Target:

* Hand-painted
* Illustrated
* Storybook-inspired

Avoid:

* Photorealism
* Heavy weathering
* Grunge
* Excessive noise
* Dark dungeon textures

The application should feel like:

"A beloved family adventure journal."

Not:

"A dungeon inventory screen."

---

# Wear and Aging

All materials should appear gently used.

Think:

* Well-loved storybook
* Family game board
* Adventure journal

Avoid:

* Ancient relics
* Heavy damage
* Ruined materials
* Burn marks
* Severe aging

Squire should feel cared for.

---

# Final Material Summary

| Material        | Meaning             |
| --------------- | ------------------- |
| Parchment       | Information         |
| Wood            | Structure           |
| Purple Cloth    | Brand & Hierarchy   |
| Stone           | Milestones          |
| Gold            | Progress & Currency |
| Gems            | Achievement         |
| Purple + Orange | Prestige            |

Every future asset should reinforce this hierarchy.

If a material does not communicate its intended meaning, the asset should be redesigned.
