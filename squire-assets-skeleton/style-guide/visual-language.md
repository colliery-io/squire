# Visual Language

Version: 1.0

Purpose:

This document defines the symbols, motifs, metaphors, and visual vocabulary that make Squire recognizable.

Color, materials, and rendering style establish how Squire looks.

Visual language establishes what Squire looks like.

This guide defines the recurring visual elements that should appear throughout the product.

---

# Design Philosophy

Squire is a family adventure.

Not a kingdom.

Not a battlefield.

Not a fantasy war.

The visual language should reinforce:

- Growth
- Adventure
- Effort
- Achievement
- Community
- Family participation

Every recurring symbol should support those values.

---

# Core Visual Metaphors

The Squire experience is built upon three primary metaphors.

---

## Adventure Journal

Represents:

- Progress
- Memory
- Growth
- Personal history

Examples:

- Quest cards
- Achievement records
- Progress tracking
- Activity history

The adventure journal is the primary metaphor for information.

---

## Quest Board

Represents:

- Opportunities
- Goals
- Available adventures

Examples:

- Quest lists
- Daily tasks
- Weekly challenges

The quest board is the primary metaphor for work to be completed.

---

## Treasure Chest

Represents:

- Rewards
- Unlocks
- Earned opportunities

Examples:

- Reward catalog
- Reward redemption
- Achievement rewards

Treasure chests should represent possibilities rather than wealth.

---

# Primary Symbols

These symbols are considered foundational to the Squire identity.

They should appear regularly throughout the application.

---

## Shield

Represents:

- Achievement
- Accomplishment
- Recognition
- Personal growth

Uses:

- Badges
- Achievement displays
- Profile emblems

Shields are one of the primary visual anchors of the Squire brand.

Shields represent effort.

Not combat.

---

## Scroll

Represents:

- Quests
- Tasks
- Objectives

Uses:

- Quest icons
- Quest categories
- Task representations

Scrolls should feel approachable and readable.

---

## Treasure Chest

Represents:

- Rewards
- Unlockables
- Motivation

Uses:

- Reward screens
- Reward icons
- Reward categories

Treasure chests should feel exciting.

Not greedy.

---

## Banner

Represents:

- Structure
- Organization
- Hierarchy

Uses:

- Headers
- Titles
- Featured content

Banners help organize information.

---

## Coin

Represents:

- Progress
- Currency
- Earned value

Uses:

- Quest rewards
- Reward costs
- Progress indicators

Coins should feel valuable.

Not magical.

---

## Gem

Represents:

- Achievement
- Long-term consistency
- Habit mastery

Uses:

- Streak rewards
- Milestones
- Special unlock requirements

Gems should feel rare and meaningful.

---

# Heraldry

Heraldry is a core component of the Squire visual language.

Heraldry represents:

- Identity
- Achievement
- Belonging

Examples:

- Shields
- Crests
- Achievement marks
- Profile emblems

Heraldry should feel:

- Friendly
- Personal
- Celebratory

Avoid:

- Military heraldry
- War banners
- Aggressive symbolism

---

# Environmental Identity

The world of Squire is centered around a village.

Not a kingdom.

Not an empire.

Not a battlefield.

The village represents:

- Home
- Community
- Family
- Belonging

Preferred environments:

- Village square
- Library
- Workshop
- Market
- Garden
- Schoolhouse
- Guild hall
- Treehouse

These environments should feel welcoming and safe.

---

# Toy Adventuring

Adventure exists within Squire.

Combat does not.

Occasional use of:

- Wooden swords
- Practice equipment
- Toy adventuring gear

is acceptable.

These objects should clearly read as:

- Toys
- Training tools
- Imaginative play

Never:

- Real weapons
- Battle equipment
- Combat-focused imagery

---

# Final Test

Before introducing a recurring symbol ask:

Does it reinforce:

- Adventure
- Growth
- Community
- Achievement

or does it reinforce:

- Power
- Authority
- Combat
- Competition

If the answer is the latter, it does not belong in Squire.
